# I am Anti-AI

I wrote another post titled the same at the beginning of October but looking back I realized that I didn't really word things how I wanted or really explain my stance on this beyond a point or two.

This has been something I have been thinking about for months now, I don't like AI and I don't think I ever really did.

I have tons of thoughts on this, my opinion hasn't been "massively" changing but it has definitely changed, and eventually led me to down a pretty depressing spiral.

As a current post-secondary student, I want to talk about what I've noticed, what led me here, and why I've come to hate AI; for real this time because I think this is a very important discussion that people need to start having.

## My Experience

ChatGPT released November 2022, I was still in high school and didn't hear about it until I started college the following year.

All of the instructors in my first year told us not to use it because it was considered cheating, and we would risk academic misconduct if we were caught.

This slowly changed over the last 2-3 years depending on the instructor, some didn't seem to care so much anymore, some let us use it within restriction, others stood by on the no AI policies; but most of them continued with something that was extremely important, **testing our understanding**.

They tested this by having us explain it beyond just submitting a written report or assignment document. 

This could include a demonstration of the lab to the instructor and answering some questions, conducting interviews with us to discuss our papers and again answering questions, or even presentations to the entire class.

However, this semester I noticed that a lot of that is going away and on top of it, more instructors are encouraging the use of AI. In my older post I said they weren't but that changed pretty quickly in a month.

In multiple of my classes I've noticed instructors using AI generated slides and assignments, and admit to using AI to mark. The marking is where I get upset because I assumed it was unknown by the school but then I found out it was actually encouraged and outlined in a policy, never being notified to students.

Going beyond that, I had an instructor share that they use AI for everything now: brainstorming, researching, verifying said research, creating content for classes, and even providing AI with their likeness so that it could replicate them.

When this instructor was asked if they think AI would replace schools, they said no because people choose to go to schools for the human factor. 

They said this though after literally admitting to the entire room that they are a "**meat proxy**" (someone who forwards answers from AI when they are asked a question).

I had been neutral around AI for quite some time and was always being extremely limited in my own use due to the environmental impacts.

When that researcher had come out in September though saying he could no longer work at any of those companies anymore due to what they were building, I immediately cut off all use and started encouraging others to do the same.

## Impact 

Now whether that researcher's reason held any truth or not, I think it should have been an important wake up call for people to start looking into AI more as well as the general climate of the world and what's going on right now.

Within a week of hearing this I actually deleted my TikTok account and other social media accounts soon after; not enough to cut myself off but enough to start gaining a sense of myself back and protect myself from having more of my data fed into these models.

I began discussing it with classmates more, sharing stuff with friends, offering my help to others to limit their AI usage, and most importantly began researching stuff more. 

Eventually I even went to talk to a counselor about my feelings as they started to negatively impact some relationships.

Now obviously I am no expert and neither are any of the people in my life, but here is what we all learnt and started to notice:

### Environmental Impact 

People always hear about this but either don't care or undermine how much it actually affects the environment.

The water usage alone of each mass AI data center is huge, problematic, and unsustainable long term. There are other things that can be done for cooling instead yet they continue to be built this one way and in water-stressed regions.

And anyone who argues saying that a closed loop water cooling system actually eliminates water consumption also doesn't understand that water is used in multiple ways for data centers but because of how places typically do environmental assessments they actually miss that information.

Along with that, the amount of energy they use for a single prompt is 10x the required energy used for a regular internet search. And in some areas where data centers are being built, they estimate that power grids actually can't sustain adding them on either.

The pollution from AI data centers goes beyond greenhouse gas emissions and water though, they also emit noise pollution that can be very harmful for the surrounding ecosystems and neighborhoods.

Lastly, a point that not only fits under here but multiple of the other categories: governments are ignoring their people, fast tracking the building process, and sometimes even breaking laws that protect the environment or their people, to build these mass AI data centers.

That last point alone should be enough of a wake up call to people, our governments are selling our planet out to these massive tech giants who don't care about it whatsoever and would rather replace our home, people, and the beauty of nature, with robots.

### Promotion of Anti-Intellectualism and Decline in Cognitive Abilities

Time and time again AI comes back to this: the more people use it the more they depend on it because they lose out on abilities like reading comprehension, problem solving, critical thinking, and being able to remember and recall information.

This is one of my biggest issues with it as well, especially as you watch more companies shove it into their services and applications. Even Google is getting rid of their search to replace it solely with Gemini instead.

### Ethical Considerations

### Replacing Humans 

This issue itself ends up boiling down beyond AI, people found a "cheaper" way to get things done so they are getting rid of employees and adding the work onto the ones that are left but unfortunately we live in a society where people just can't work.

Many of the CEOs that own AI companies have also come out recently saying that they expect it to eventually replace humans entirely (wild thing to say about 8+ billion other people); people like Sam Altman (OpenAI), Elon Musk (SpaceXAI), and Dario Amodei (Anthropic) just to name a few.

If this is the way they view 8+ billion lives, why are governments letting them get away with so much? Governments should be protecting them because at the end of the day what good does your money have for you if no one exists?

### Lack of Transparency and Honesty

How many times this year have you heard of an AI model doing something it wasn't supposed to? Or if you did hear about it, how many times later did you find out it was actually worse than the companies originally said?

They are extremely reckless with what they are building and how they go about testing it, often with-holding major details from any incidents that make it public, and the fact so many incidents happen though is also a major red flag.

Along with this though on the other side is fearmongering, at times overplaying certain situations but providing very few details for us to actually figure out for ourselves if it is as bad as it seems.

It's not just the massive tech giants though, it's also various other companies implementing AI into their systems or enforcing their employees to use it.

My school for example, creating a new policy that allows instructors to use it and encouraging them to but not informing students of this change or requiring instructors to disclose it is very disrespectful but also kind of shady. 

### Shady CEOs

Not so much an issue with AI itself but an issue with the people who built the biggest models out there and are in-control of it.

## Final Verdict

Until the day that all of these are addressed and changed, I will firmly stand on the side of being anti-AI. I like creating art, building things, learning, and just experiencing life. 

One thing I have continuously repeated is that art is the process but that also goes beyond art, living is the process. What is living without falling and making mistakes, what is living without some sort of process? 

Everyone is so insistent on getting an end result that everyone forgot about everything in-between and just looking for shortcuts, eventually cutting out, in my personal opinion, the most fun part.
