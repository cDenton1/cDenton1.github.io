# Bandit Writeup - OverTheWire

**NOTICE**: This writeup is going to be broken up into multiple posts; one for each level. This is where you will be able to access the posts for each level, and read about my overall experience playing OverTheWire Bandit. Breaking down the overall writeup like this will make it much easier for me to write for each level, easier for people to find specific levels they may need, and avoid an extremely long post. 

Over the past year, I kept hearing about this cybersecurity game called, OverTheWire: Wargames. Curious, once I finally had some free time, I sat down to give it a try and see what it was all about. For anyone looking to learn more and/or practice their cybersecurity skills, this is a really fun way of doing that and I’d recommend it. I’ve only played through Bandit so far, which is aimed at beginners, but it gave me a chance to practice commands I already knew, and learn a lot more.

## What is OverTheWire: Wargames?

It’s a collection of cybersecurity practice material called, wargames, which are offered and put together by the OverTheWire community and are meant to help people learn and practice cybersecurity concepts through fun, game-like levels. There are 16 wargames in total, 12 of which can be played through their website, and another 4 which are available for download. Each wargame focuses on a different area of cybersecurity, offering a variety of difficulties and concepts to learn about. All of these and more information related to each one can be found on their website, [https://overthewire.org/wargames/](https://overthewire.org/wargames/).

---

## My Experience

I started OverTheWire Bandit back on February 18th of this year, and finally beat it May 17th. I sat down to play through it five different times, each time spending anywhere from 30 minutes to 2 hours. The game isn't long, but if you're unfamiliar with any of the concepts or commands, you will spend majority of the time trying different things, and by no means is this a bad thing. There were levels I could easily speed through, and others where I had to spend some time reading up on different commands and concepts; but that's the whole point.
