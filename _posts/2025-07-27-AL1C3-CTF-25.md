# AL1C3 1N PWND3RLAND 

On July 26th, SAIT hosted their summer CTF for 2025, themed after Alice in Wonderland. This was the first CTF I competed in solo, and I found it to be a really good learning experience. I finished 2nd amongst the solo competitors with 1125 points and solving eight challenges.

This post is going to be less of a walkthrough on each challenge I solved and more of a reflection on the event and anything memorable from it.

As much as I love cryptography challenges, I had used this CTF as an opportunity to try challenges in a wide variety of categories instead of solely focusing on cryptography. I did attempt them throughout, but I also did rotate between quite a few challenges in general, so as to try as many as I could. 

Cryptography isn't the only category I enjoy, but I find it's one of my strongest. Which means I tend to focus on those challenges early on and for the majority of competition time. Competing solo actually gave me the chance to exlpore areas I tend to miss or avoid.

Reverse engineering challenges were something I had practiced a lot prior to CyberSci Nationals back in June, but during that competition, I hadn't spent a lot of time on them. Seeing there were four in this CTF, I had worked on those for quite some time. I successfully solved three out of the four, only scoring points for two of them due to an error that I will explain later on.

Even in categories I practice and compete in regulary, I was still learning new things. For example, the OSINT challenge, _Where's That Grin?_, introduced me to the site [what3words](https://what3words.com/embedded.fizzled.trial). A website that has the world divided into small squares, where each square is given a unique combination of three words. I had never heard of this site before but I found it so fitting for the competition theme and honestly hope to see it come up more in the future.

#### Das Auge sieht ein Geheimnis

Translated from German, _The eye sees a secret_. As you may have guessed, this challenge for the most part was in German. It gave us some English to start, but the main description of the challenge and the provided file, which was a three page PDF, was in German. 

Translating them pointed us in the direction of what we were looking for, for the flag, but it also threw in quite a few red herrings that honestly had me lost at times.

In the challenge we are asked in German, "with which words did their candidate initiation ceremony end?". In the PDF there was the mention of several tasks which included three simple decryption related puzzles, none of which led towards the flag. 

This is when I learned that files can be embedded into PDFs. I was so focused on visuals, I almost didn't even consider this. I attempted to use tools for extracting, such as _binwalk_ and _pdfdetach_, but they either failed or only got me part of the way. 

I wrote a quick script in Python to extract from the PDF using an offset I found through binwalk, and you can find the file here: [extractFromPDF.py](https://github.com/cDenton1/Extra-Files/blob/main/ip-AL1C3/extractFromPDF.py).

Now that's as far as I got in this challenge. In the ZIP folder there was a hidden folder, and a couple layers down there was the following image:

![](/assets/images/the_final_answer.png)

I had absolutely no idea where to go when it came to decoding it. Speaking with the challenge creator afterwards, it was encoded with the copiale cipher, which was created by a secret German society. By using [https://www.dcode.fr/copiale-cipher](https://www.dcode.fr/copiale-cipher), we can see the flags value was **THECANDIDATEANSWERSYES**.
